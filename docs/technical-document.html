1Summary

T&G BnB is the owner's own booking website for their holiday homes in Kenya. Guests browse the homes, see the full price for their dates, and pay online in Kenyan shillings through Paystack (M-Pesa or card). The owner and their team run everything from a dashboard: homes, calendar, bookings, refunds, guests, messages, reviews and money.

Kenya onlyEvery home has a Kenyan address. Prices in KES. Times in Nairobi time.
No feesPrice = nights × nightly rate + cleaning charge. Nothing added at checkout.
142 testsBackend tests all pass, with lint and security scans clean.

Highlights

  • Server-side pricing. Every price is worked out on the server from the home's settings, so a guest can never change what they pay.
  • No double bookings. The database itself refuses overlapping stays, even when two guests pay at the same moment.
  • Local payments. M-Pesa and card through Paystack's secure checkout, with refunds sent from the dashboard.
  • Instant booking or requests, chosen per home, with Flexible, Moderate or Strict cancellation policies.
  • A complete owner dashboard: homes, calendar, bookings, refunds, guests, inbox, reviews, finances, team and an activity log.
  • Built for phones first, accessible by keyboard and screen reader, with the privacy features Kenya's Data Protection Act asks for (data download, account closure, consent).

6Booking and payment

The server works out every price from the home's own settings. The browser sends only the home, the dates and the number of guests, so a guest cannot change what they pay.

Steps

  1. The guest picks dates and guests. The server checks the dates against the home's rules: not in the past, within the minimum and maximum nights, within the guest limit, the home published, and no booked or blocked nights in the way.
  2. The server creates the booking as awaiting payment and holds the dates for 15 minutes. It saves the price at that moment: nightly rate, nights, cleaning charge and total.
  3. The guest goes to Paystack's checkout page and pays by M-Pesa or card.
  4. Paystack tells the server the payment succeeded, in two ways: the guest's browser returns to the site and the server asks Paystack to confirm it, and Paystack also sends a signed "paid" message (webhook) even if the guest closes the page. Either way the result is the same, and a payment is only ever counted once. The server checks that the amount and currency match what was charged.
  5. Instant booking homes confirm straight away and the guest gets a receipt. Request homes become a request; the owner has 24 hours to accept or decline. A request that gets no answer is declined automatically and refunded in full.

What stops double bookings

The database itself refuses two active bookings (awaiting payment, requested or confirmed) on the same home with overlapping nights. Even if two people press "Pay" at the same moment, only one gets the nights. The database also checks that the total always equals nights × nightly rate + cleaning charge.

Unpaid holds and late payments

  • A background task runs every minute. It frees holds that weren't paid within 15 minutes and declines requests that are past 24 hours.
  • If a payment arrives after its hold expired and the nights are still free, the booking still goes through. If someone else has taken the nights by then, the payment is marked as a refund due, and the owner sees it in Bookings and Finances.

7Cancellations, date changes and refunds

Cancellation policies (chosen per home)

PolicyGuest gets back
FlexibleEverything, if they cancel before the day of check-in.
ModerateEverything at least 5 days before check-in; half after that.
StrictHalf at least 7 days before check-in; nothing after that.
  • Guests can cancel until the day before check-in. They see the refund before they confirm.
  • A request the owner hasn't accepted yet is always refunded in full if the guest cancels it.
  • When the owner cancels a stay or declines a request, the guest gets everything back.

Changing dates

Guests (before check-in) and the owner (until check-out) can move a confirmed or requested stay to other free dates. The nightly price stays the same. A shorter stay refunds the difference. A longer stay is refused; the guest books the extra nights separately.

How a refund is paid

Cancelling, declining or shortening a stay works out the refund and marks it as due on the payment. The owner then settles it on the booking's page in one of two ways:

  • Refund through Paystack: the site asks Paystack to send the money back to the guest's card or M-Pesa.
  • I refunded it in Paystack: for a refund the owner already sent from the Paystack dashboard; the site only records it.

Either way the guest is told, the Finances page updates, and the action is logged.

9Security and privacy

  • Prices and rules on the server. The browser cannot set a price, a refund, a booking status or a role.
  • Access checked on every request. Every owner action checks that the signed-in person is on the team. Guests only ever see their own bookings, messages and data.
  • Sign-in. Passwords are at least 8 characters and stored only as bcrypt hashes. The session cookie is signed, hidden from page scripts (httpOnly), HTTPS-only in production, and SameSite=Lax. The API also refuses changes sent from other websites (an Origin check), which blocks cross-site form tricks. The live site forces HTTPS.
  • Payments. Card and M-Pesa details never reach the site; Paystack handles them. Paystack's "paid" messages are accepted only with a valid signature (HMAC-SHA512 with the secret key), and amounts are checked against the booking.
  • Limits on repeated attempts from the same IP address, for example: sign-in and sign-up 10 per 3 minutes, password reset emails 5 per 10 minutes, verification emails 3 per 10 minutes, bookings 20 per 10 minutes, messages 30 per 10 minutes, contact form 5 per 10 minutes.
  • Uploads are team-only and limited by type and size. Photos are re-encoded and resized on upload.
  • Private details: a home's check-in instructions and wifi details are shown only to a guest with a confirmed stay.
  • Audit log of every team action, with the person and IP address.
  • Data protection (Kenya, Data Protection Act 2019). There are privacy and terms pages, and a cookie notice. Marketing consent is opt-in and recorded with a date. Guests can download all their data. Closing an account removes the person's name, email, phone, photo and sign-in, but keeps their bookings and payments for the owner's tax and dispute records.
  • Checks on every change: a static security scan (Brakeman) and a check of known-vulnerable libraries (bundler-audit) run in GitHub Actions with the tests. Both are clean.

10Hosting and settings

How a new version goes live

  • API: Render rebuilds from backend/Dockerfile on every push to the main branch that changes backend/. On start it runs any database updates (migrations). The first start on an empty database loads the schema and the demo homes.
  • Website: deployed with vercel deploy --prod from client/. The Vercel project can also be connected to the Git repository to deploy on every push. client/vercel.json forwards /api/v1 and /rails to the Render API. If the API address changes, update that file.
  • Background jobs (emails, expiring holds) run inside the API process (SOLID_QUEUE_IN_PUMA=1), so no separate worker is needed.

11Going live on the owner's accounts

The system is complete. Going live means connecting it to the owner's own accounts and production hosting. Each step is configuration only; no code changes are needed.

12Planned enhancements

The system is built so these can be added without reworking what exists.

  • Seasonal and weekend pricing, length-of-stay discounts and extra-guest charges.
  • Calendar sync (iCal) with Airbnb and Booking.com, so nights booked elsewhere close automatically.
  • Team roles, for example staff who manage bookings but not finances.
  • Extending a stay from the booking page, charging only the extra nights.
  • Automated website tests (Vitest and Playwright), alongside the existing backend tests.

13Developer guide: backend

Rails 8.1 in API mode, Ruby 4.0.2, PostgreSQL (UUID keys; pgcrypto, citext, btree_gist). JSON under /api/v1. Uploaded files are served under /rails (Active Storage). Time zone: Nairobi.

Run it

cd backend
bin/setup --skip-server     # gems, databases, migrations
bin/rails db:seed           # amenities, 12 Kenyan demo homes, dev accounts
bin/rails server            # http://localhost:3000, health check GET /up

In development the seed creates a team account admin@tgbnb.test and a guest guest@tgbnb.test, with the passwords in db/seeds.rb. These work on a developer's machine only.

Checks (all must pass; CI runs them on every change to backend/)

bin/rails test              # Minitest, 142 tests
bin/rubocop                 # style
bin/brakeman --no-pager     # security scan
bin/bundler-audit           # vulnerable gems

Tests use a fake Paystack client and a fake Google check, so they never call outside services.

Conventions

  • Money is whole integers in cents (*_cents), always KES. Bookings save their price at the time of booking.
  • Errors are always { "error": { "code", "message", "details"? } }. Validation errors use code validation_failed, with field messages in details.
  • Lists page with ?page=; responses include the next page number when there is one.
  • Team actions call audit(action, target, summary) to write the Activity log.
  • Key rules are also enforced in the database: no overlapping active bookings, totals that match, dates in order, valid statuses.
  • In development, expired holds are not cleared automatically (the schedule runs in production only). Run Booking.expire_stale_holds! in the console if needed.
